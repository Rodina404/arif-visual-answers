For GSM8K, which is a benchmark of math word problems, the paper shows that the effect of chain-of-thought prompting depends strongly on the size of the model, meaning that with small models there is almost no difference and sometimes it is even worse, but as the model grows the difference becomes very large, and for example with PaLM, which comes in several sizes, the small 8B version reached 4.9% accuracy with standard prompting and 4.1% with chain of thought, so chain of thought actually hurt a little, while the 62B version went from 9.6% to 29.9%, and the large 540B version went from 17.9% to 56.9%, which is a very large jump that gave the model the best known result on GSM8K at the time and even surpassed a finetuned GPT-3 with a verifier, and the paper calls this phenomenon an emergent ability, meaning an ability that only shows up once a model reaches a certain scale, because smaller models produce chains of thought that look fluent but are illogical and therefore lead to wrong answers, and the same pattern appears for other models such as LaMDA and GPT-3 in the tables in the appendix.
